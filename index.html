01 / Purpose & planning

Why this project,
and how I planned it.

↑ Project index

I wanted to apply the skills from my training to a project I could plan, develop and explain myself.

I chose the deliverables by researching electrical VDC workflows, reviewing BIM and VDC job listings, and consulting a VDC professional. The scope brought together modeling, spatial coordination, drawing sheets, panel schedules, prefab planning, quantities and record updates responding to a simulated field change.

Snowdon Towers gave me an existing building to work within. I registered seven Autodesk source models and selected a vertical slice around grids 7–9 / A–D: Utility P05 at Parking, Retail 109 at Level 1 and the overhead building systems. That area offered an equipment room, a vertical transition and an overhead distribution route within a manageable boundary.

02 / The electrical model

From the equipment room
to Retail 109.

↑ Project index

The electrical work connects a Parking-level equipment layout to a focused raceway scope at Retail 109.

I started a new electrical model in Revit, then linked Autodesk’s architectural and HVAC models so I could see the building around my work and coordinate where everything would go. I then placed the equipment, identified devices, built circuit relationships and schedules, and developed the conduit and cable-tray geometry.

The equipment room and vertical route

Two panels, a disconnect and a transformer form the equipment-room scope. Conduit connections link the enclosures, and a riser from P-208-1 reaches junction box J1 above. Equipment positions, mounting and connector alignment developed together as I worked through the bends.

Overhead distribution at Retail 109

The overhead scope includes three parallel 2-inch EMT conduits at 4-inch centers, their trapeze supports, and a 12 × 4-inch wire-mesh cable tray. Six receptacles provide the local device scope. Onward distribution at the modeled limits is recorded as an assumed continuation.

How the early layouts developed

The S07 equipment record captures the initial placement. S08 records six receptacles and two junction boxes. The scope changed to one junction box in S10, and the later model and drawings reflect that decision.

03 / Coordination & troubleshooting

Working through
the model.

↑ Project index

The routes became clearer through view control, modeling adjustments and formal clash review.

Plan, section and 3D views answered different questions. I checked the electrical work beside linked HVAC, then used Navisworks to review hard intersections. Three documented stories show how the model developed.

CR-01 / Navisworks clash review

Clarifying the concealed wall route

The first review identified related riser and elbow intersections at the wall face. I moved the risers fully into the wall, adjusted the elbows, refreshed the model and reran the tests.

The three original elbow pairs became Resolved. The remaining concealed-riser wall intersections were Approved as intentional for this educational review.

CR-02 / Revit equipment troubleshooting

Making room for the connections

Early equipment spacing and mounting made the conduit connections difficult to route. I reworked the arrangement and connector alignment so the bends and enclosure connections could be modeled clearly.

This is a documented Revit troubleshooting story. It records the change in geometry and placement.

CR-03 / Revit junction-box troubleshooting

Refining the J1 transition

I refined J1’s placement and conduit approach, organizing a straight upward connection with the bend at the overhead transition. The earlier and revised views preserve the development of that connection.

S10: investigating a warning before deciding what to change

The route was initially obscured by linked models, and section-box clipping made some elements appear to protrude. I isolated the electrical work, compared selected-segment properties and their reference levels, and checked the junction-box family and warning list. Two warnings identified unused family ports, which I recorded as accepted for that milestone.

06 / Revision & closeout

Revising the supports
and closing the record.

↑ Project index

For FC-01, I simulated a field change to improve the support layout.

The earlier model used sparse, representative supports. I kept the original markup as a record, adjusted the support spacing, and added a support at the cable tray elbow. The updated model has six conduit supports and eight tray supports, labeled H1 through H14.

Updating the affected records

I carried the change through E101, E103, IS1.01, PF1.01 and MTO-01. I updated the model views, installation information and quantities, corrected the hanger BOM to match six assemblies, and issued the Rev01 drawing package with change annotations on those five sheets.

Checking the record issue

I refreshed the Navisworks review after the change. The final report records 13 hard tests with 0 New and 0 Active results, alongside 23 Approved and 13 Resolved findings. The Approved findings record model conditions I intentionally accepted in this review. Some geometric intersections remain.
